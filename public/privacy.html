Privacy Policy

Updated 17 November 2026

What PeakCut does

PeakCut Studio is an open-source video clipping workspace. Its local engine processes videos and transcripts on the computer running it. The default local AI setup does not send transcripts to a paid AI API.

Social account connections

When you choose to connect a social account, the platform handles sign-in. PeakCut stores the account identifier, display name, and authorization tokens in the local engine data folder. App secrets and account tokens are not placed in the public GitHub repository or Vercel frontend. The HTTPS callback passes a one-time authorization result to your paired local engine.

Publishing and video links

When you publish, the selected platform receives the clip and the caption and settings required for that post. Instagram retrieves clips through signed, expiring HTTPS links served from the local computer through Cloudflare. TikTok uploads video bytes directly. These services process network requests under their own policies.

Browser and hosting data

The browser remembers a private local-engine pairing key in local storage and temporarily stores connection state in session storage. Vercel serves the frontend and receives normal website requests, including network metadata such as IP addresses. PeakCut does not sell social access tokens or use them for advertising.

Your control and retention

Disconnect an account in Settings → Connected accounts to remove its locally saved connection. You may also revoke PeakCut in the social platform’s connected-app settings. Disconnecting does not delete posts already published. Local videos, transcripts and project files remain until you delete them; copies and backups must be removed separately.

Questions

For privacy questions, contact the maintainer through the public project repository at github.com/sasukefrontenddev/peakcut-studio. Do not include credentials, private videos, or personal information in public issues. The app is currently maintained as a local-first open-source project.
